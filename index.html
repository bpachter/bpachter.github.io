Nullius

Per-atom density evidence for PDB ligands
TypeScriptBinaryCIF decoderWebGL point cloudsRDKit WASMAlphaFold1.9M-row indexNo backend
When a protein structure is published, every atom looks equally certain. It isn't — the experiment measures an electron-density cloud and a person decides where the atoms go. This shows, atom by atom, which parts of a molecule the measurement actually supports. Paste a target and get every ligand ever modelled into it, ranked worst-evidence-first from a precomputed 1.9M-row index of the whole PDB — one request, ~50 ms, no backend. Open one and the electron density is fetched and decoded in the browser by a from-scratch BinaryCIF reader verified byte-identical to Mol*, then sampled at every atom. An AlphaFold band sits beside the experimental one, deliberately never merged with it: a measurement and a prediction are different kinds of claim. The app re-runs 15 checks against known values on load and refuses to display numbers it cannot reproduce.

Thessa

Supply-chain knowledge graph
Ontology designReact + TypeScriptSEC EDGAR · USAspendingProvenance-firstMIT
A knowledge graph of who depends on whom across space, defense, AI infrastructure and critical minerals — 41,330 typed nodes across 10 types — 15,184 of them organizations, the rest programs, products, segments, people and materials — joined by 52,011 typed edges across 20 relation kinds, and 78,226 federal awards worth $1.98T, of which 97.5% resolve to a recipient already in the graph. The hard part isn't the join, it's deciding what earns a place: a filer must name any customer above 10% of revenue and state the dollar figure, so that edge carries an audited number, while a press release carries only an assertion. They are stored on different trust rungs — 2,803 edges from filings and federal records against 1,185 press-derived — and refuted claims stay in the audit ledger rather than quietly disappearing. Free, no paid tier, open source.

Open-Weight Agent Safety

Prompt-injection research on local LLM agents
AI safety evalsOllamaQuantized open weightsFactorial designSingle 24 GB GPU
Measurement infrastructure for indirect prompt injection against the agents people actually run — quantized open-weight models on one consumer GPU. The outcome is binary and mechanically observable (did the model call a destructive tool no task requires?), so thousands of unattended trials mean something without an LLM-as-judge. The structural finding: attack success is not one number — ASR = P(delivered) × P(obeys | delivered) × P(executes | obeys) — and benchmarks reporting the product miss models that look safe only because they never called the carrier tool: one model recorded 0 successful attacks in 258 trials without ever being attacked once. Three-armed controls (attack / clean / benign) keep the causal claim honest.

03 · Standards I wrote

Owning standards is easy to claim and hard to show, so here are the artifacts rather than the adjective. Each is a rule I wrote, enforced in code, on a public repository you can open — the private version of this work is the part I cannot show you.

01

A written vocabulary of refusal

A verification service returns twelve named failure modes — undetected_rollup, duplicate_facts, bare_name_merge, substance_demotion and the rest — never a bare "failed". Each name carries its own definition, and the whole vocabulary is served as data from GET /failure-modes, so the same names become the review-queue filters and the metric dimensions. One vocabulary, four runtimes, no shared library.

OntologyLaw.cs
02

Publication is structurally impossible without a verdict

Not policy — three mechanisms at once. The API is read-only, so no update endpoint exists that could quietly move a proposal to approved. The approve action returns HTTP 409 unless the verdict passes. Every gate field is read-only in the review UI, so a curator cannot type a verdict into a form. Rejection, meanwhile, is a free bulk action: refusing never needs a verdict. The asymmetry is the rule.

views.py
03

Trust is derived, never stored

A stored confidence score drifted so far from measured accuracy that the high-confidence band audited worse than the band beneath it. I replaced it with a five-tier taxonomy computed at read time from the evidence itself — best source class, inference method, latest audit verdict. Attach one better record and the claim is promoted on its next read: no rewrite sweep, no stale labels, and one function every consumer asks.

kgTrust.ts
04

One canonical list, and a repair that refuses to guess

Merging two nodes moves every reference off the loser. If the list of referencing tables misses one, nothing errors — rows just stay attached to a node the graph now treats as noise. Two tables were missing, and the cost was specific: the produces edge from Cameco, the largest publicly traded uranium producer on earth, did not follow its merge, so the critical-minerals layer quietly lost its uranium miner. The list is now a single shared constant with a regression test, and the repair reads the merge logs rather than inferring a target by name — a stranded row whose merge is in no log is reported, never rehomed on a guess.

repairStrandedNodeRefs.ts
05

Every bulk change is a dry run until you say otherwise

Migrations, merges, retypes and retirements all default to dry-run, with the read-only database flag wired to the same switch so a rehearsal physically cannot write. Each is reversible and leaves a JSON log beside the database. The discipline pays for itself in refusals: adversarial review under it killed 5 of 32 proposed demotions and 4 of 13 proposed merges, each of which pointed the wrong way.
