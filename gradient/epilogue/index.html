What you know now

You know what a weight is — a dial — and that learning is nothing grander than billions of dials feeling their way downhill. You know the tower: words becoming coordinates, attention letting every word watch every other, one skill — guess the next token — wearing a trench coat of temperature sliders. You know why it's fast (the engine room), how it grew hands (the loop), why it pauses to think (verifiable rewards), and what we find when we finally look inside (features, waiting for a dial of their own).

And you know the other half, the part most explainers skip: that none of it matters to a business until the knowing is engineered too. Things as nodes. Relationships as typed, receipted edges. Identity defended at the merge desk. Agents that propose, verifiers that doubt, gates that hold, humans who decide how much to delegate — and a flywheel that compounds it all while everyone sleeps.

← Loops with receipts, gates with humans. That's the whole doctrine.

Part I wrote backpropagation out by hand, for one fixed network, and checked it against brute force. What has been asking you to take it on trust ever since is the general case: that loss.backward() — the line every framework is built around — works for any expression you type, and is still arithmetic you could have written yourself. So here is that engine whole, in plain Python: a tape that records every operation, a reverse walk that hands each node its share of the blame, an nn-shaped layer, an optimizer, and a ring it has no business being able to find. Then every gradient is checked against brute force again, because a claim this size should be measured, not asserted.

Every dial you touched in these thirty-two chapters is a real dial, simplified but never faked: the perceptron used Rosenblatt's actual rule, the XOR net trained by real backpropagation, the GRPO rounds really did grade themselves on a curve, and the join tax was honest arithmetic. The frontier will keep moving — it always does — but it will keep being made of these parts: loops, dials, edges, receipts, and someone's hand on a gate. Now you know where to look.

And the last thing on the last page, which you can now simply read: the same ring model in real PyTorch, and again in real TensorFlow. It cannot run here — there is no browser build of either library — but nothing in it should surprise you. zero_grad, backward, step: you wrote all three a few paragraphs ago.
